Chapter 1: What Is a Real Estate Auction?
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1.1 Definition and Basic Concepts

The assets sold in real estate auctions can come from legal proceedings, banks, 
insurance companies, businesses or private individuals.

A real estate auction is a category of real estate asset sales, and in this type 
of transaction there is no direct negotiation between a buyer and a seller. In 
the case of judicial auctions, the sale is made through a legal process, and the 
judge will homologate or reject the purchase. In auctions, interested parties make 
bids in competition, in which the best offer will be homologated.

1.3 Why Properties Go to Auction

The main factor is crisis. Brazil has not yet recovered from the economic crisis 
that left millions unemployed. We had the COVID-19 pandemic, we had a post-COVID 
recession, and this reduced the number of real estate launches. Those with money 
began to look for used properties with better payment conditions. This was the 
starting point for the growth of the auction market.

Brazilians discovered this possibility and started making bids to buy their own 
homes paying much less. Furthermore, many financial institutions loaded with 
repossessed properties that needed to be sold facilitated negotiation, spread 
payments and, in many cases, even financed the winning bid with terms almost as 
long as a mortgage and reduced rates.


1.4 The History of Auctions in Brazil

The real estate auction is based on Law number 9,514, of November 20, 1997, which 
provides for the Real Estate Financing System, institutes the fiduciary alienation 
of real property and provides other provisions.

The Real Estate Financing System (SFI) aims to promote real estate financing in 
general, under conditions compatible with the formation of respective funds.

Chapter 2: The Legal Foundation of Auctions
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

2.1 Law 9,514/97: The Real Estate Financing System (SFI)

Law 9,514/97 is the foundation of the real estate auction market in Brazil.

Article 1: The Real Estate Financing System (SFI) aims to promote real estate 
financing in general, under conditions compatible with the formation of respective 
funds.

Article 2: The following may operate in the SFI: Caixa Econômica Federal, 
commercial banks, investment banks, banks with real estate credit portfolios, 
real estate credit companies, savings and loan associations, mortgage companies 
and, at the discretion of the National Monetary Council (CMN), other entities.

Article 3: Real estate credit securitization companies, non-financial institutions 
established as joint-stock companies, shall aim to acquire and securitize these 
credits and issue and place on the financial market Certificates of Real Estate 
Receivables (CRI), being able to issue other securities, conduct business and 
provide services compatible with their activities.


Second auction under the out-of-court procedure: if the first auction has no bid equal to or above the property value set in the contract, a second auction is held within 15 days. In it, the bid must be equal to or above the full debt, expenses and legal charges (art. 27, §§ 1 and 2, of Law 9,514/97). The Superior Court of Justice treats a bid below 50% of the appraised value as a vile price (AgInt nos EDcl no REsp 1,931,921/SP, 2021), a prohibition that REsp 2,096,465/SP also applies to out-of-court enforcement.

2.2 Law 13,465/2017: Updates and Modernizations

Law 13,465/2017 brought important updates to the auction market, especially with 
regard to extrajudicial auctions, which now fall under this law in conjunction 
with Law 9,514/97.


2.3 The Role of the National Monetary Council (CMN)

The CMN is the body responsible for defining which entities may operate in the 
SFI, as provided in Article 2 of Law 9,514/97.


2.4 Certificates of Real Estate Receivables (CRI) and Securitization

Real estate credit securitization companies aim to acquire and securitize these 
credits and issue and place on the financial market Certificates of Real Estate 
Receivables (CRI). This mechanism allows real estate credits to be transformed 
into tradable securities on the financial market, increasing liquidity in the 
sector.


2.5 The Constitution and Property Rights

The real estate auction, although a form of forced execution, respects the right 
to property provided for in the Constitution, as it occurs within a legal process 
that guarantees due process and full defense to the debtor.

Chapter 3: Two Types of Auctions
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The auction can be held in judicial or extrajudicial form.

3.1 Extrajudicial Auction

In the extrajudicial auction, governed by Laws 9,514/97 and 13,465/2017, the 
extrajudicial auction of real estate is executed in cases of default of at least 
three installments of a property sold to the financial institution with fiduciary 
alienation.

How it works:

1. The citizen bought an apartment with financing
2. Did not pay three installments
3. Went to the extrajudicial auction process

The process:

The bank will send to the debtor, via the real estate registry office, written 
notification to pay the overdue and adjusted installments, according to a 
spreadsheet that will accompany the notification. The debtor will normally have 
15 days to pay the debt in full and at the office.

Otherwise, the financial institution may pay the ITBI (Real Estate Transfer Tax) 
and secure the property in its favor. Within 30 days of consolidation, the 
property may be put up for auction and is purchased.

How to prevent the auction:

The only way to prevent the property from being auctioned is through a court 
action informing the judiciary the reasons for the default, and proposing to pay 
the full debt at once, depositing the amounts in a court-linked account attached 
to the proceedings, or in case the creditor accepts an agreement on the 
installment plan of the debt.


3.2 Judicial Auction

It occurs through a court action to collect debt on the property itself, which 
can vary between condominium fees or unpaid property taxes.

How to suspend a judicial auction:

In this case, suspension of the auction will only occur with payment of the full 
debt or if the creditor accepts an agreement on the installment plan of the debt.


3.3 The Difference Between 1st and 2nd Call

In judicial auctions, there are two calls:

1st call: The property is offered at the appraised value. If there are no bids, 
it proceeds to the 2nd call.

2nd call: The property is offered again, with a minimum bid set by the judge and the notice. Law prohibits so-called "vile price" sales, and bids far below the appraised value may be annulled. Check the floor that applies to your case with a lawyer.

It is in the 2nd call that opportunities often appear, but the discount depends on the notice and the legal floor against vile price.

Chapter 4: Who Can and Cannot Participate
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

4.1 Legal Persons and Natural Persons: Basic Requirements

Any person over 18 years old, natural or legal, can participate in an auction.

To participate, it is necessary:

• To appear at the location where the auction will be held as stated in the notice, 
  carrying personal documents
• Or participate online by doing early registration on the auctioneer's website


4.2 Restrictions in Article 890 of the Code of Civil Procedure

The following may not bid on the assets and situations indicated in Article 890 of the Code of Civil Procedure:

• Guardians, curators, executors, administrators and liquidators, regarding the assets entrusted to their care and responsibility

• Attorneys-in-fact, regarding the assets whose administration or sale they are in charge of

• Judges, members of the Public Prosecutor's Office and Public Defender's Office, court clerks and other court staff, in the locality where they serve

• Auctioneers and their agents, regarding the assets whose sale they are in charge of

• Attorneys of either party


4.3 Minors: Is It Possible?

No. Participation in an auction requires full legal capacity, that is, being 
18 years old.


4.4 Foreigners: Special Rules

Foreigners can participate in auctions in Brazil, but must observe specific rules, 
which will be detailed in Chapter 26 (Bonus).

Chapter 7: How to Make a Bid
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

7.1 In-Person Auction: Required Documents and Procedures

To participate in an in-person auction, it is necessary:

1. Attend the location indicated in the notice
2. Carry personal documents (ID, CPF)
3. Be attentive to the auctioneer's conduct
4. Make verbal bids during the bidding


7.2 Online Auction: Early Registration and Bidding Strategies

To participate in an online auction:

1. Register early on the auctioneer's website
2. Submit required documentation
3. Deposit a security deposit (usually 5% of minimum bid)
4. Make bids through the platform on the scheduled date


7.3 Strategies to Win at the Lowest Possible Price

1. Set your maximum bid before the auction — and don't exceed it
2. Participate in auctions with few bidders — less competition
3. Make bids in the final minute — avoids inflating the price before time
4. Don't let emotions carry you — discipline is the key


7.4 What to Do If You Lose the Bid

If you lose the bid, don't be discouraged. The auction market offers opportunities 
every day. Analyze what went wrong and prepare for the next one.


7.5 Psychology of the Auction: How Not to Be Carried Away by Emotion

The auction is an emotional environment. The auctioneer is trained to create a 
sense of urgency and competition. To avoid falling into this trap:

• Have your maximum bid defined and don't change it during the auction
• Remember: there will always be another property
• Take a deep breath before making a bid

Chapter 8: The Role of the Auctioneer
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

8.1 Who They Are and What Their Functions Are

The auctioneer is the professional responsible for conducting the auction, 
receiving bids and declaring the winner.


8.2 Requirements to Be an Auctioneer

The profession of auctioneer will be exercised by means of registration granted 
by the commercial boards of the Federal District, the states and the Acre 
territory.

To be an auctioneer, it is necessary:

• To be a Brazilian citizen
• To be in the enjoyment of your civil and political rights
• To be over 25 years old
• To be domiciled in the place where you intend to practice the profession for 
  more than five years
• To have proven integrity with the presentation of identity card and negative 
  certificates from the registry offices
• To present a negative certificate of lawsuits or executions filed against him 
  in the Federal Civil Court and local corresponding to his domicile and relating 
  to the last five years

May not be auctioneers:

• Those who were previously removed from this profession, except if they were 
  removed at their request
• Bankrupts not rehabilitated
• Rehabilitated persons when bankruptcy has been classified as negligent or 
  fraudulent


8.3 Guarantee and Legal Responsibilities

The auctioneer, after being properly qualified before the commercial boards, is 
obligated, by court order of the same boards, to provide a guarantee in money or 
in a federal government bond.


8.4 How to Check If an Auctioneer Is Trustworthy

1. Consult the auctioneer's registration at the Commercial Board
2. Check if there are lawsuits against him
3. Research the auctioneer's reputation on the internet
4. Ask for references from other investors

Chapter 9: The Purchase — The Moment of Truth

9.1 What Happens After the Winning Bid

After the winning bid, the buyer must:

1. Pay the bid value (or down payment, if paid in installments)
2. Await homologation by the judge (in judicial auctions)
3. Register the purchase at the real estate registry office


9.2 Homologation by the Judge: What It Means and How Long It Takes

In the case of judicial auctions, the sale is made through a legal process, and 
the judge will homologate or reject the purchase. Homologation can take from a 
few days to several months, depending on the complexity of the case.


9.3 The Payment: Cash, Installment or Financed?

Many financial institutions have facilitated negotiation, spread the value of the 
purchase and, in many cases, even financed the winning bid, with terms almost as 
long as a mortgage and with reduced rates.

9.5 Financing After the Auction

In bank auctions, such as those of Caixa, the notice may allow financed payment. Rules that usually appear:

1. Down payment: in Caixa auctions, the down payment is usually at least 5% of the minimum sale price, paid within 2 business days after approval. Check the notice.
2. Financing and FGTS: allowed only when the property listing says so. It is not a general rule.
3. Financed percentage: promotional material mentions up to 95%, but the actual amount depends on the notice and the bank's approval. Treat any figure as a starting point, not a guarantee.
4. Auctioneer fee: usually about 5% of the property value, paid by the buyer. Check the notice.
5. Second auction: if the first auction has no winning bid, the second auction takes place within 15 days, with a minimum bid that can be lower, but never below 50% of the appraised value (art. 27, §2, of Law 9,514/97, as amended by Law 14,711/2023).

Before relying on financing, confirm with the bank the required income, documents, property appraisal, maximum term and fees. Because the property is held under fiduciary sale, a buyer in default loses the right to pay off the debt and recover the property once ownership is consolidated: according to the Brazilian Superior Court of Justice (Theme 1,288, REsp 2,126,726/SP), once ownership is consolidated under Law 13,465/2017 there is no longer a right to cure the default; the debtor keeps only a right of first refusal (art. 27, §2-B, of Law 9,514/97).

The figures in this text come from press reports and 2026 auction notices. Always confirm them in the notice of each property.

Chapter 10: Taking Possession — How to Take the Property
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

10.1 Unoccupied Property: The Fastest Path

If the property is unoccupied, taking possession is immediate. The buyer can take 
possession as soon as payment is made and the purchase is homologated.


10.2 Occupied Property: How to Proceed

If the property is occupied, the situation is more complex. In this case:

1. Check in the notice if occupancy is reported
2. Try an out-of-court settlement with the occupant
3. If necessary, file an action for taking possession


10.3 Taking Possession: Out-of-Court or Judicial

The path depends on how the auction was held.

In an out-of-court auction, governed by Law 9.514/97, the buyer has the right to take possession once the auction certificate is recorded in the property registry (art. 30). If the occupant does not leave, a possession action may be filed. While the occupant stays, an occupancy fee of 1% per month, calculated on the property value stated in the contract for out-of-court auction, is due from the auction to the date of possession (art. 37-A, as amended by Law 14.711/2023). A lawsuit challenging the auction, without a decision suspending the procedure, generally does not prevent possession, according to decisions of state courts. This is not a guarantee: check your case with a lawyer.

In a judicial auction, possession follows the expropriation rules of the Code of Civil Procedure. Deadlines and remedies differ and must be checked with a lawyer.

Warning: the occupant may allege that the procedure was invalid. Read the notice of auction and the property registration before bidding. Also check whether the notice proves that the debtor was personally notified of the auction dates: courts debate when the lack of personal notice voids the procedure, and the debtor's clear awareness has been considered sufficient in some cases. Confirm with a lawyer.

12.5 Taxation of the Sale: When Tax Applies to the Gain

When you sell the property for more than the acquisition cost, the difference is a capital gain, subject to personal income tax at progressive rates: 15% up to R$ 5 million of gain; 17.5% from R$ 5 million to R$ 10 million; 20% from R$ 10 million to R$ 30 million; and 22.5% above R$ 30 million. There are two important exemptions:

1. A single property sold for up to R$ 440,000, with no other sale in the last 5 years (art. 23 of Law 9.250/95).
2. Sale of a residential property with the full proceeds reinvested in another residential property in Brazil within 180 days (art. 39 of Law 11.196/2005). Partial reinvestment, in construction or land, does not guarantee the exemption.

The taxable gain is calculated in the GCAP system and paid by DARF. Even when exempt, the sale must be reported in the tax return. This text is informational and does not replace the advice of an accountant.

Chapter 13: The Mathematics of Profit
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

13.1 ROI (Return on Investment): How to Calculate

ROI (Return on Investment) measures profit relative to total investment.

Formula: ROI = (Profit / Total Investment) × 100


Practical Example:

• Property bought for R$ 180,000
• Costs (renovation, taxes, etc.): R$ 50,000
• Total investment: R$ 230,000
• Sale for R$ 320,000
• Profit: R$ 90,000

ROI = (90,000 / 230,000) × 100 = 39.1%


13.2 ROE (Return on Equity): The Definitive Indicator

ROE (Return on Equity) measures return on own capital invested.

Formula: ROE = (Profit / Own Capital) × 100

PART V — THE DANGER
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Chapter 18: The Real Risks (That No One Tells You About)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

18.1 Occupancy Risk: The Hidden Cost

The property may be occupied. If it is occupied, the possibility of judicially 
requesting taking possession may take some time, in addition to more legal costs. 
Eviction can take from 6 months to 2 years.


18.2 Legal Risk: Debts and Incomplete Documentation

The property may have hidden debts (property taxes, condominium fees, liens) that 
may become the responsibility of the new owner. It is essential to analyze the 
property's registration at the real estate registry office.


18.3 Renovation Risk: Budget Exceeded

The property may be in poor condition, requiring renovations that exceed the 
planned budget. Always add a 30% reserve for unexpected expenses.


18.4 Liquidity Risk: Property Stuck

The property may take more than 12 months to sell, tying up the investor's capital.


18.5 Competition Risk: Bid Too High

The bid may be inflated by competition, eroding profit.


18.6 Macroeconomic Risk: Interest Rates, Inflation and Crisis

The real estate market can be affected by economic crises, rising interest rates 
and inflation.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Chapter 19: How to Avoid Scams and False Promises
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

19.1 The "Property with 94% Discount" Scam

Be wary of promises of miraculous discounts. Real discounts usually range from 
30% to 60% of market value, not 94%.


19.2 Be Wary of "Miracle Course" Sellers

Many course sellers promise to get rich with auctions, but have never participated 
in an auction in their lives.


19.3 How to Verify Auctioneer's Integrity

1. Consult the auctioneer's registration at the Commercial Board
2. Check if there are lawsuits against him
3. Research reputation on the internet


19.4 Consulting Negative Certificates and Records

Always request negative certificates of lawsuits or executions filed against the 
auctioneer at the Federal Civil Court and local corresponding to your domicile 
and relating to the last five years.


19.5 Fake Websites and Phishing: How to Protect Yourself

• Always verify the auctioneer's website domain
• Don't click on suspicious links
• Confirm the website address directly with the auctioneer

Chapter 26 (Bonus): How Foreigners Can Invest in Auctions in Brazil
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

26.1 Fundamental Requirements: CPF and Documentation

The first and most important step for any foreigner interested in participating 
in an auction in Brazil is to obtain a CPF (Individual Taxpayer Registry). This 
is the tax identification number mandatory for any real estate transaction in the 
country.

How to obtain:

The process can be carried out through the Brazilian consular network in the 
country of residence of the investor. Registration normally takes 2 to 3 weeks 
to complete.

Basic required documentation:

• Valid passport (with at least 6 months validity)
• CPF registration certificate
• Proof of address (electricity, water bill or bank statement)
• Power of attorney with specific powers, if using a representative in Brazil 
  (must be notarized and apostilled)

It is also recommended to obtain the RNE (National Registry of Foreigners), 
although it is not strictly necessary for a single purchase.


26.2 Opening a Bank Account and Foreign Exchange

Real estate transactions in Brazil are mandatory in Brazilian Reais (BRL). 
Therefore, the investor needs to plan the transfer of funds to an account 
in Brazil.

Bank account: It is advisable to open an account at a Brazilian bank to enable 
the transfer of funds.

Foreign exchange: Foreign exchange operations must be carried out through 
institutions authorized by the Central Bank of Brazil, and the investor must be 
prepared to provide documentation of the origin of the funds to comply with 
compliance requirements.


26.3 Auction Participation: Online and In-Person

The vast majority of real estate auctions in Brazil today occur online, which 
facilitates the participation of foreign investors.

Registration: The interested party must register on the auctioneer's platform 
before the auction date. This registration requires the presentation of a CPF 
and identity documentation.

Security deposit: Many auctions require the deposit of a security amount (usually 
5% of minimum bid) to credential the participant.


26.4 The Notice and Due Diligence (Critical Analysis)

"Due diligence" is the most critical stage and cannot be neglected. Unlike 
traditional purchases, the property at auction is sold "as is".

The Notice: It is the document that governs the auction. Must be read carefully. 
It contains all information about the property, debts, occupancy and payment 
conditions.

Registration Research: The property's registration with the Real Estate Registry 
Office is the property's birth certificate. It is essential to analyze this 
document to verify the chain of ownership, the existence of liens, mortgages or 
other debts that "follow" the property.


26.5 Specific Restrictions: Rural Areas, Border and Marine Properties

While the acquisition of urban real estate is relatively free for foreigners, 
Law 5,709/71 imposes significant restrictions on rural properties and areas of 
national interest.

Rural Properties:

• Acquisition by foreign natural persons is limited to 50 indefinite exploitation 
  modules
• For areas between 3 and 50 modules, INCRA authorization is required
• Brazilian companies with a majority of foreign capital are treated as foreign 
  for these purposes

Border Zone (up to 150 km from border): Purchasing urban or rural properties in 
these areas by foreigners depends on prior authorization from the National 
Defense Council.

Coastal Properties (Marine Land): Acquisition also faces restrictions.

Non-compliance with these rules can lead to nullity of the transaction.


26.6 Alternatives and Structures: Right to Surface, Partnership with Brazilian Partner

To bypass restrictions or optimize investment, alternative structures exist:

Right to Surface: Allows the foreigner (surface holder) to build or plant on 
property owned by a third party (grantor), without acquiring full property of 
the land. It is an alternative to economically exploit the property without 
facing restrictions on land purchase.

Partnership with Brazilian Partner: A joint venture with a Brazilian partner can 
enable transactions in the rural sector, diluting foreign control.

Usufruct: Another alternative is the constitution of a real property right of 
usufruct, which guarantees the foreigner the right to use and enjoy the property 
without being the full owner.


26.7 Specialized Legal Advice

A foreign investor should not act alone. Legal and bureaucratic complexity makes 
specialized legal advice support not just recommended, but essential.

Process Coordinator: A local law firm can act as a "command center", coordinating 
legal, exchange, due diligence and property registration aspects of the deal.

Prior Preparation: Legal advice can prepare the investor before even defining 
the asset, ensuring that the investment structure, documents and banking channels 
are ready for execution.


26.8 Summary Checklist for Foreign Investor

1. Obtain CPF
2. Open a bank account in Brazil (or plan foreign exchange transfer)
3. Select an urban property to avoid complex restrictions
4. Perform complete due diligence: analyze the notice, property registration, 
   debts and occupancy situation
5. If necessary, seek authorizations (INCRA, CDN) in advance
6. Hire local legal advice to conduct the process
7. Set aside resources for costs beyond the bid (ITBI, fees, possible debts)
8. Participate in the auction with a predefined bid limit, avoiding getting 
   involved in emotional disputes

Appendix A: Full Text of Law 9,514/97 (Most Relevant Provisions)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Law No. 9,514, of November 20, 1997

Provides for the Real Estate Financing System, institutes the fiduciary alienation 
of real property and provides other provisions.

Article 1: The Real Estate Financing System (SFI) aims to promote real estate 
financing in general, under conditions compatible with the formation of respective 
funds.

Article 2: The following may operate in the SFI: Caixa Econômica Federal, 
commercial banks, investment banks, banks with real estate credit portfolios, 
real estate credit companies, savings and loan associations, mortgage companies 
and, at the discretion of the National Monetary Council (CMN), other entities.

Article 3: Real estate credit securitization companies, non-financial institutions 
established as joint-stock companies, shall aim to acquire and securitize these 
credits and issue and place on the financial market Certificates of Real Estate 
Receivables (CRI), being able to issue other securities, conduct business and 
provide services compatible with their activities.

Appendix D: How to Check the List of Official Auctioneers
════════════════════════════════════════

The list of official auctioneers is kept by the Commercial Registry (Junta Comercial) of each state. An auctioneer may only operate if registered with the board of the state where they work.

Where to check (addresses verified in October 2026):


RJ: JUCERJA, www.jucerja.rj.gov.br (publishes the list of registered public auctioneers)

MG: JUCEMG, jucemg.mg.gov.br/pagina/139/leiloeiros-oficiais

AL: JUCEAL, juceal.al.gov.br/servicos/leiloeiros

ES: JUCEES, www.jucees.es.gov.br/servicos/leiloeiros/

SP: JUCESP, address to be confirmed before publication
Other states: search for "Junta Comercial do [state] leiloeiros" on the official state government website.

How to check whether an auctioneer is official:


1. Ask for the full name and the registration number with the Commercial Registry.

2. Check that number on the state board's website and confirm the name and number match.

3. Confirm the registration is active, without a license or suspension.

4. Ask for the registration certificate issued by the Commercial Registry, a document required in accreditation notices.

5. Be wary of PDF lists and websites that do not appear on the official list. JUCEMG reports it has detected fake lists and sites using auctioneers' names and the board's brand.

Note: the number of auctioneers per state is set by the commercial registries and may change. There is no fixed national number, and this appendix gives no figures.

Appendix E: Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. Is it Safe to Buy a Foreclosed Property?

Purchasing a property at auction is very attractive. However, this transaction 
can become complex for those without the legal knowledge necessary to conduct the 
business. To ensure a good deal and peace of mind in the purchase, it is essential 
to have specialized advice on property auctions.


2. What Is the Average Discount in Auctions?

Discounts vary by property, auction stage and notice. In practice, they usually range from 30% to 60% of market value.


3. Can I Finance the Purchase?

Many financial institutions have facilitated negotiation, spread the purchase 
value and, in many cases, even financed the winning bid, with terms almost as 
long as a mortgage and with reduced rates.


4. What Happens If the Property Is Occupied?

If the property is occupied, the possibility of judicially requesting taking 
possession may take some time, in addition to more legal costs.


5. How Long Does It Take to Sell?

The average for sales is 12 months.


6. What Is the Average ROI?

Considering all costs involved in acquiring a property and sale expenses, it is 
possible to achieve, in a single deal, an ROI between 50% and 60%.


7. Can Foreigners Participate in Auctions in Brazil?

Yes. Foreigners can participate in auctions in Brazil, but must obtain a CPF and 
observe specific restrictions for rural properties and border areas.
